# SacPassport booklet

**Status:** Idea and outline, September 30, 2026. Not scheduled. Parked as item 3 in the [Backlog](Backlog.md).

A printed SacPassport booklet, with a custom rubber stamp at each festival.

## The idea

The site does the first, slow part: it steers people toward cultures they have not tried. The booklet is the next step. It puts a newcomer at a festival with a reason to talk to someone.

The larger goal is on the organizer side. Most cultural festivals are built by and for people who already know the culture. The food has no labels, the dances are not introduced, and nobody is assigned to explain anything. That works for the community. A newcomer walks the grounds, eats something they cannot name, and leaves.

When passport holders show up asking the same questions, organizers start to see that they need tour guides and an experience designed for newcomers. The booklet is how that feedback reaches them.

## What the booklet does not do

It does not list what to eat, watch, or buy. SacPassport cannot know what a festival will serve that year. Elk Grove Lunar New Year did not serve bánh tét. A printed list is wrong on the day and turns the page into a checklist the visitor can fail.

Content about the event comes from the visitor, who asks someone, or from the organizer, who fills in the Host's Picks box. The stamp pages print only prompts. Festival names and usual months appear once, on the calendar and map.

It also does not expect anyone to visit every culture. A realistic pace is about two festivals a month. The booklet holds about eight months of that, and a full booklet is replaced with the current edition. See [Editions and refills](#editions-and-refills).

## Booklet outline

Near real passport size: 3.5 × 5", the size novelty passport printers stock (a US passport is 88 × 125 mm, about 3.5 × 4.9"). One stamp and its notes per page.

28 pages, stapled: a 4-page cover and 24 inside pages, the same count as a standard US passport. Page counts must be multiples of 4.

| Pages | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Outside front cover | Passport styling and edition year |
| Inside front cover | Traveler's pledge |
| 1 | Holder ID page |
| 2–3 | Year at a glance (spread) |
| 4–5 | Map (spread) |
| 6 | How it works |
| 7–22 | Stamp pages: 16 pages, one festival each |
| 23 | Trails and milestones |
| 24 | Autographs and notes |
| Inside back cover | "If found, please return to" and an emergency contact line |
| Outside back cover | QR to sacpassport.com, the check-in, and milestone redemption |

At two festivals a month, 16 stamp pages last about eight months. Renewing is part of the design: a holder picks up a fresh booklet once or twice a year, and each renewal is another visit to a stamp table.

The calendar and map each get a two-page spread (7 × 5") because 57 festival names or venue pins do not fit on one page at this size.

### Front

- **Cover.** Styled like a real passport: dark cover, a seal printed in gold-toned ink, "SacPassport," and the edition year. Full-color print, no foil. The cover color changes with each edition.
- **How it works.** Find the stamp table, one stamp per festival per booklet, what milestones earn, and where to get the next booklet when this one is full.
- **Traveler's pledge.** Ask before you take a photo. Try one new thing. Say thank you in the host's language. Follow the host's lead in sacred spaces.
- **Holder ID page.** Photo or drawing box, name, home neighborhood, languages spoken, "my family's roots," and stamps carried over from earlier booklets.
- **Year at a glance.** Each festival's usual month for that edition, listed by month across the spread, with a QR code for current dates. It is a menu, not a checklist.
- **Map.** Venue pins across Sacramento, Yolo, Placer, and El Dorado counties, numbered to the calendar, with a small Bay Area inset.

### Stamp pages

Every page is open. Any festival stamps the next empty page, the way any country stamps any visa page. Alternates, pop-ups, and restaurant partners use the same pages.

Each page has, inside a 0.25" margin:

- **Top row.** The stamp zone on the left: a 1.6" circle with a faint outline, for a 1.5" stamp, and room for organizer initials if the stamp is missing. The Host's Picks box on the right, about 1.25" square, for the organizer's sticker or second stamp for that year: what to try, what to watch and when, what not to miss.
- **Prompts below,** at about 8 pt with room to write by hand. Each one sends the visitor to a person:
  - It's about ______. I asked ______.
  - I ate ______. It's called ______.
  - I watched ______.
  - A word I learned: ______.
  - I didn't understand: ______.
  - Went with ______. Rating ☐☐☐☐☐

"I didn't understand" is the most useful line in the booklet. It lets the visitor admit confusion without embarrassment. Across a season, those answers are the evidence organizers need to see.

Page backgrounds rotate through textile, tile, and craft motifs from different cultures, like the watermark art on real visa pages. The motif is decoration. It does not assign the page to a culture.

### Trails

Trails are based on the event, not the menu, so they stay true whatever gets served. They carry across booklets.

- **New Years Around the World.** Hmong New Year, Tết, Chinese New Year, Losar, Nowruz, Songkran, Khmer New Year, Lao New Year, Thingyan, Tamil New Year, and Ethiopian New Year.
- **Dance Floor.** Five festivals where you joined a dance, not just watched one.
- **Continents.** One stamp from each world region.
- **Find a dumpling.** An open prompt: write down whatever dumpling you found and what it was called. Nothing is prelisted.

### Back

- **Trails and milestones.** One tally row per trail, and sticker spots at 5, 10, and 25 stamps and at every continent. Counts include stamps carried over from earlier booklets, so the 25 mark always spans more than one booklet.
- **Autographs and notes.** Performers, dancers, festival chairs, and sketches.
- **Redemption.** On the outside back cover, as a QR code for milestone rewards.

No running "words I learned" or "things I ate" lists. The stamp-page prompts already collect both, and those pages go to stamps instead.

## Editions and refills

- **One edition a year.** Each edition gets a new cover color and art, an updated calendar and map, and new trails where the calendar changed.
- **A full booklet gets the current edition.** Pick it up at any stamp table or a cPALSs booth. Nobody waits for a new year to keep going. At 16 stamps, most holders renew within the same edition year at least once, so their second booklet is often the same edition. That is fine.
- **Old booklets stay with the holder.** The stamp table marks the full one "COMPLETE" on the cover, like a cancelled passport, and hands it back. A shelf of past editions is the record of someone's years of festivals.
- **An older edition never expires.** Keep filling it after a new edition comes out. Only the calendar goes stale, and the QR code covers that.
- **Stamps carry over.** The new booklet's ID page records how many stamps came from earlier booklets, so milestones and trails keep counting. The old booklet is the proof.

## Stamp kit

What a participating festival receives:

- The festival's own stamp. Self-inking, 1.5" (38 mm) round to fit the stamp zone, with the festival name. Stamp pages are not labeled, so the stamp names the event. No year in the rubber, so the stamp lasts. The table writes the date beside it, or uses a small date stamp.
- Refill ink for the stamp.
- A tabletop sign: "SacPassport stamps here. First time? Ask us anything."
- A Host's Picks template, to be filled in a week before the event and printed as 1.25" square stickers, or as a second stamp. Three short lines is the limit at that size.
- The organizer guideline below.
- A small stock of the current edition, for first-time holders and for anyone whose booklet is full.
- A "COMPLETE" stamp for full booklets.
- A thank-you note and the QR code for the newcomer report.

The stamp table is the wedge. Hosting a stamp means putting a person at a table, and once passport holders arrive that person answers questions all day. It becomes the festival's first welcome desk without anyone having to pitch the idea.

## Guideline for festival organizers

*Designing for newcomers who carry a SacPassport.* Written to be handed to organizers as is.

Some of the people at your festival this year have never been to one like it. They came with a SacPassport booklet, and they want to understand what they are seeing, not only collect a stamp. A few small choices turn a confused visitor into one who comes back and brings friends.

1. **Staff the stamp table with someone who likes to explain.** Visitors will ask what a dish is, when the dancing starts, and what the festival celebrates. That table is your welcome desk. Post the day's schedule on it.
2. **Fill in Host's Picks a week ahead.** Name one thing to try, one performance to watch and its time, and one thing not to miss. You know this year's festival. We don't.
3. **Name the food.** A card at each booth with the dish name, one line on what it is, the price, and whether it is cash only.
4. **Introduce each performance.** One sentence from the MC in English before each act: what it is, where it comes from, what to watch for.
5. **Offer a way in.** A dance the crowd can join, a craft table, a guided walk at set times. Newcomers remember what they did more than what they watched.
6. **Explain etiquette kindly.** If shoes come off, heads get covered, or photos are not welcome somewhere, say so plainly on a sign or at the table. Most newcomers want to get it right.
7. **Read your newcomer report.** After the event, SacPassport sends what passport holders told us: what they loved, what confused them, and what they would ask next time.

You don't need all seven in the first year. A staffed stamp table and a Host's Picks card already put you ahead of most festivals.

## Feedback loop

- **QR check-in on every stamp page and on the stamp table sign.** Three questions: Did anyone explain things to you? What confused you? Would you come back? Under 30 seconds.
- **Newcomer report for each organizer, once a year.** How many passport holders came, what they asked most, and what confused them. For example: most wanted to know when performances started, and several didn't know food was cash only.
- **Participation tiers, shown on sacpassport.com:**
  - *Listed:* on the site.
  - *Stamp host:* staffs a stamp table.
  - *Host's Picks:* fills in the newcomer card each year.
  - *Guided:* scheduled walks or cultural ambassadors.
  - *Newcomer-designed:* food labels, narrated performances, a way to join in, and a newcomer map.
- **First-timer friendly badge** on cards at the Guided tier and above. Organizers want it, and visitors pick events by it.

## Pilot: Elk Grove Lunar New Year

EGLNY, February 13–14, 2027, is the first test because cPALSs runs it:

- Run the stamp table as a welcome desk.
- Fill in EGLNY's own Host's Picks card.
- Schedule two or three Tết guide walks led by volunteers.
- Collect check-ins and write the first newcomer report.

After that, the pitch to other organizers is what EGLNY did and what newcomers said, not an idea.

The pilot needs only the booklets, one EGLNY stamp, a table sign, and a Host's Picks card. Stamp kits for other festivals, COMPLETE stamps, refills, rewards, and the participation tiers wait until a second festival signs on.

### Spec to send for quotes

- 3.5 × 5" finished, saddle stitched, 28 pages: a 4-page cover and 24 inside pages.
- Inside: 70 lb uncoated text, full color both sides.
- Cover: 14 pt, full color, matte lamination on the outside. No foil.
- Two rounded corners on the outer edge, for the passport look.
- Quantities: 100 and 250 for the pilot. 500 and 1,000 for later.
- One hard-copy proof before the run.

Questions for each shop:

- Can your stitcher staple a 5" spine cleanly? Can I see a booklet near 3.5 × 5" that you have produced?
- What does a reorder of 250 cost, for refills of the same edition?
- Turnaround from approved proof.

## Open questions

- **Paper.** Uncoated, so stamp ink dries without smearing. The benchmark prices use 70 lb uncoated text. Test with the actual self-inking stamps, and check that stamps do not show through to the next page.
- **Price and distribution.** First booklet free at festival stamp tables, sold, or both. Whether a refill costs anything. Who holds stock between events.
- **Print run.** How many copies of each edition, given that refills of the current edition come out of the same run.
- **Rewards.** What milestones earn, and who funds it.
- **Stamp design.** Commissioned per festival, or a shared SacPassport frame with the festival name and a culture motif inside.
- **Organizer outreach.** Which festivals to ask first, and when. Stamp kits have to arrive before each organizer's own planning deadline.
